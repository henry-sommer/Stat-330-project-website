#create the cv_rmse function
library(rsample)
cv_rmse <- function(formula, data, folds) {
  results <- lapply(seq_len(nrow(folds)), function(i) {
    train <- analysis(folds$splits[[i]])
    test <- assessment(folds$splits[[i]])      #maybe somebody add some comments about what is going on here
    fit <- lm(formula, data = train)
    predictions <- predict(fit, newdata = test)
    sqrt(mean((test$wins - predictions)^2))
  })
  mean(unlist(results))
}
folds <- vfold_cv(nfl_combined, v = 5, repeats = 20)

cv_results <- data.frame(
  Model = "Model 1",
  CV_RMSE = cv_rmse(formula(nfl_mod_final), nfl_combined, folds)

  • Changed — How we adapted our data to fit our focus:

Our AI did not seem satisfied with our predictors and data in general. So we decided to combine two datasets together so we could have more predictors that could potentially help explain our response, “wins”. We didn’t want to leave any important defensive statistics out at the beginning. That is why the model above is slightly different from the one in our AI prompt.

  • Rejected or corrected — AI suggestions we didn’t like:

Our AI seemed very convinced that we had a serious multicollinearity problem. Looking at the VIFs, however, there wasn’t as big of an issue as AI predicted. AI also suggested adding other defense variables. Unfortunately, including too many of the variables would make our analysis too complicated and unfocused.

Connection to the final case study:

Our observations have each football team 23 different times—because of seasons from 2000-2022. Since our AI was so concerned about independence, it suggested that we use a “Linear Mixed-Effects Model”. This model will give a random intercept for each time out of a normal distribution. AI says this model will mitigate the risk of non-independence between our observations. This extension will appear on our final page where we check the “LINE” assumptions since it will help us verify the independence assumption.

  1. 2nd check — This time we use the “Durbin-Watson Test.”

For our second check of our Linear Mixed-effects Model we would like to do something called a “Durbin-Watson Test”. This is a test that can also look at independence. Since we concluded that independence wasn’t a big issue in part one, we want to check how accurate that claim is against another method. Here are the hypotheses of the Durbin-Watson test:

\(H_0\): There is no correlation between the residuals (the residuals are independent)

\(H_A\): The residuals are autocorrelated
